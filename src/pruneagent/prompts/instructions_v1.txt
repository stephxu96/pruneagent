Your job. You are pruning a language model so its weights fit within a memory
limit of {limit} MB, while keeping as much quality as possible. Quality is
perplexity on held-out text; lower is better. A result above the memory limit
does not count. The model is never retrained or fine-tuned: quality lost in
any step is not recovered later.

Your result will be compared with FLAP, an existing method, cut to the same
memory limit. FLAP's perplexity at this limit is {flap_ppl}. The unpruned
model's is {dense_ppl}. Compare yourself with FLAP only once you are at the
limit; before that your model is larger and should score better than FLAP.

How rounds work. Each round, propose 1–3 candidates. Each candidate is a list
of (layer, method) choices, applied together to the current model, one
fixed-size step per choice. Code measures each candidate's real perplexity.
A candidate is rejected if {rejection_rule}. Of the rest, code keeps
{selection_rule}. If all are rejected, nothing changes and the round is used.
You have {rounds_left} rounds left. {stop_rule}

Candidates are your experiments: make them test different ideas, not small
variations of one idea. Small steps let you re-measure before committing
further. You can return to an earlier kept plan, but a later step cannot undo
the damage of an earlier one.

The three methods (one step each):
- narrow_mlp: removes 10% of the layer's original feed-forward width. Frees
  about {x} MB.
- remove_attention_group: removes one of the layer's {n_groups} attention
  groups. Frees about {y} MB.
- drop_layer: removes the whole layer. Frees about {z} MB.

Steps free different amounts of memory. Judge a step by perplexity increase
per MB freed, not by perplexity increase alone.

Your evidence, from least to most current:
- Quick tests: estimated damage for each layer and method. In our tests they
  were reliable for choosing which method suits a layer.
- Measured damage: real perplexity after one step on each layer alone,
  measured on the unpruned model. Use it for choosing which layer to prune.
  It ignores how layers interact, and it becomes less accurate as the model
  changes.
- Round results: real perplexity of every candidate so far, including
  rejected ones, measured on the model as it was that round. When they
  disagree with the evidence above, trust the round results.

Where you are.
- Memory now: {current_mb} MB. Still to free: {mb_to_free} MB.
- Perplexity now: {current_ppl}.
- Previous rounds: {history}

Rules. {limits: at most N layers dropped, minimum widths, etc.} A candidate
